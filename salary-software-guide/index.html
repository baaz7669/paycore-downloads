How to choose salary software for a small business

Updated October 2026 · 6 minute read

If you're comparing payroll tools or searching for the best salary software, this guide covers what to check, what each type of tool costs, and when each one makes sense. We make PayCore, so we've also been clear about when it isn't the right fit.

Short answerThe best salary software for you covers your monthly payroll steps (salaries, overtime, leave, loans and payslips), stays affordable as you hire, and keeps your data where you're comfortable. Price per employee is the biggest difference between tools over time.

Your three options

Excel

Cost: free.

Fine for a few staff on fixed salaries. Overtime, leave and loans mean more formulas, and one broken cell changes someone's pay without anyone noticing.

Cloud subscription

Cost: monthly fee plus a charge per employee.

Works from any computer or phone, often with employee self-service and, in some countries, automatic tax filing. The bill grows with your team, every month.

Desktop, one-time

Cost: paid once.

Installed on your own computer and works offline. No monthly bill and your data stays with you, but it's tied to that computer and usually to Windows.

Features to check before you buy

  • Salary structure: basic pay plus the allowances you actually use (housing, transport, phone, food).
  • Monthly calculations: overtime, bonuses, unpaid leave and deductions worked out automatically.
  • Loans and advances: installments taken from salary each month, with a way to pause or record early repayment.
  • Payslips and reports: PDF payslips with your company name, plus an Excel export for your accountant or bank.
  • End-of-service and bank rules: gratuity at the rate your country uses (see our gratuity calculator), and a fixed bank-transfer amount if you follow a wage protection system (WPS).
  • Currency and language: your currency with the right decimals (OMR, KWD and BHD use three), and Arabic if your team needs it.
  • Your data: where it's stored, how you back it up, and how you get it out if you leave.
  • Support: who answers when payroll is due tomorrow and something looks wrong.

Questions to ask any vendor

  1. What will I pay per year if my team doubles?
  2. Can I export all my payroll history if I stop using it?
  3. Does it handle my country's end-of-service and bank transfer rules?
  4. Can I see it run a full month of payroll before I pay?
  5. Who do I contact for support, and how fast do they answer?

Common questions

What is the best salary software for a small business?

There isn't one best tool for everyone. The best choice is the one that covers your monthly payroll steps, stays affordable as you hire, and keeps your data where you're comfortable. Cloud tools suit teams that need access from several places; a desktop tool like PayCore suits an office that runs payroll on one computer and doesn't want a monthly bill.

Is there free salary software?

Excel templates are free, and most paid tools offer a free trial or demo. Fully free payroll software is rare and usually limited to very small teams or basic features. Check what happens to your data and price once you pass the free limit.

Is Excel enough for payroll?

For a handful of staff with fixed salaries, Excel can work. It gets risky once you add overtime, unpaid leave, loans or end-of-service, because every formula is maintained by hand and one broken cell changes someone's pay. Payroll software does those calculations the same way every month and keeps a history.

What does salary software cost?

Subscription tools usually charge a monthly base fee plus a fee per employee. For 20 employees, the published prices we checked in October 2026 range from roughly $457 to $2,028 a year. One-time desktop software is paid once instead.
